Language Toolkit

Advantage & Disadvantage Language

Phrases and linkers for discussing benefits and drawbacks

Introducing Advantages

One of the main advantages is that... A major benefit of... is... The most significant advantage is... One positive aspect is that... Perhaps the greatest advantage is...
e.g. "One of the main advantages of studying abroad is that students can experience different cultures."

Introducing Disadvantages

On the other hand, a key disadvantage is... However, the main drawback is that... One notable disadvantage is... A significant downside is that... Nevertheless, a major drawback is...
e.g. "However, the main drawback of online learning is that students may feel isolated."

Balancing Both Sides

While it is true that..., it is also important to consider... Although there are benefits, there are also drawbacks. Despite the advantages, we must also consider... On balance, the advantages outweigh the disadvantages. Even though... , it should be noted that...

Useful Linkers

Furthermore Moreover In addition However On the other hand Nevertheless Conversely Whereas

Problem & Solution Language

Phrases and linkers for identifying issues and proposing solutions

Describing Problems

One of the most serious problems is... A major issue facing... is... A growing concern is that... One of the biggest challenges is... An increasingly common problem is...
e.g. "One of the most serious problems in large cities is air pollution caused by heavy traffic."

Proposing Solutions

One possible solution would be to... This problem could be solved by... An effective way to address this is... The government could tackle this by... A practical solution would be to...
e.g. "One possible solution would be to invest more in public transport systems."

Evaluating Solutions

This approach would be effective because... If this solution were implemented,... This would help to reduce / improve... As a result of this measure,...

Useful Linkers

Therefore As a result Consequently For this reason In order to Thus Hence Accordingly

Cause & Effect Language

Phrases and linkers for explaining reasons and consequences

Introducing Causes

One of the main reasons for... is... This is mainly due to... The primary cause of... is... This situation arises because... A key factor contributing to... is...
e.g. "One of the main reasons for the rise in obesity is the popularity of fast food."

Describing Effects

As a result, ... This leads to / results in... One consequence of this is... This has a significant impact on... The effect of... is that...
e.g. "As a result, more young people are suffering from health problems at an earlier age."

Linking Cause and Effect

Because of this,... Due to the fact that... Since / As... Owing to... This means that...

Useful Linkers

Therefore Consequently As a consequence Thus Hence For this reason As a result Because of

Compare & Contrast Language

Phrases and linkers for drawing similarities and differences

Showing Similarities

Similarly,... In the same way,... Like..., ... also... Both... and... share the characteristic of... One thing they have in common is...
e.g. "Similarly, both traditional and online education aim to provide students with valuable knowledge."

Showing Differences

In contrast,... Unlike..., ... While / Whereas... On the other hand,... ... differs from... in that...
e.g. "In contrast, online learning offers more flexibility but less social interaction."

Making Comparisons

Compared to / with... ...is more/less... than... ...is not as... as... The main difference between... and... is...

Useful Linkers

However On the contrary Whereas While Similarly Likewise In contrast Alternatively

Essay Organisation Guide

Learn how to organise your opinion essay effectively. A well-organised essay follows a clear 4-paragraph structure.

General 4-Paragraph Opinion Essay Structure

This structure applies to all opinion essays at B1+ level
1
Introduction (Paragraph 1)

Begin with a general statement or hook about the topic. Provide brief background information. End with a clear thesis statement that presents your opinion or outlines what the essay will discuss. Do NOT include arguments here.

2
Body Paragraph 1 (Paragraph 2)

Start with a clear topic sentence introducing your first main point. Support it with explanations, examples, or evidence. Use linkers to connect your ideas. End with a sentence that links to the next paragraph or reinforces your point.

3
Body Paragraph 2 (Paragraph 3)

Start with a topic sentence for your second main point (or the opposing view). Develop it with supporting details and examples. Use appropriate transition language. Connect back to your overall argument.

4
Conclusion (Paragraph 4)

Restate your opinion using different words. Briefly summarise your main points. End with a final thought, recommendation, or a look to the future. Do NOT introduce new arguments here.

Organisation by Relationship Type

Each essay type has its own recommended way of organising body paragraphs.

Advantage & Disadvantage

Block or point-by-point organisation
1
Introduction

Introduce the topic and state that there are both advantages and disadvantages to consider.

2
Body 1: Advantages

Present 2-3 key advantages with supporting explanations and examples.

3
Body 2: Disadvantages

Discuss 2-3 key disadvantages with supporting explanations and examples.

4
Conclusion

Summarise and give your overall opinion: do advantages outweigh disadvantages, or vice versa?

Problem & Solution

Describe problems, then propose solutions
1
Introduction

Introduce the issue and signal that problems and solutions will be discussed.

2
Body 1: Problems

Describe 2-3 significant problems related to the topic, with concrete details.

3
Body 2: Solutions

Propose practical solutions for each problem mentioned. Explain how they would work.

4
Conclusion

Restate the importance of addressing the problems and the effectiveness of the proposed solutions.

Cause & Effect

Explain why something happens and what results
1
Introduction

Introduce the phenomenon or situation. Indicate that causes and effects will be explored.

2
Body 1: Causes

Explain 2-3 key causes or reasons behind the situation with evidence or examples.

3
Body 2: Effects

Discuss the consequences or impacts that result from the causes identified.

4
Conclusion

Summarise the cause-effect chain and offer your opinion or prediction for the future.

Compare & Contrast

Examine similarities and differences
1
Introduction

Introduce the two subjects being compared. State the purpose of the comparison.

2
Body 1: Similarities (or Subject A)

Discuss key similarities between the two subjects, or describe one subject in detail.

3
Body 2: Differences (or Subject B)

Discuss the key differences, or describe the second subject and highlight contrasts.

4
Conclusion

Summarise the comparison and state which option you prefer or recommend, with justification.

Key Tips for Good Organisation

  • One main idea per paragraph: Each body paragraph should focus on a single main point.
  • Topic sentences: Start each body paragraph with a clear topic sentence that tells the reader what the paragraph is about.
  • Supporting details: Follow your topic sentence with explanations, examples, or evidence.
  • Linkers and transitions: Use appropriate connecting words to link ideas within and between paragraphs.
  • No new ideas in the conclusion: The conclusion should summarise, not introduce new arguments.
  • Balanced paragraphs: Try to keep your body paragraphs roughly similar in length.
  • Aim for ~300 words: A well-developed B1+ essay is typically around 280-320 words.

Email Submission Settings

Configure email delivery so that student essays are sent directly to your inbox when they click "Submit for Review".

How to Set Up (One-Time)

  1. Go to emailjs.com and create a free account.
  2. In the dashboard, click "Email Services" → "Add New Service". Connect your email (Gmail, Outlook, etc.) and note the Service ID (e.g. service_abc123).
  3. Go to "Email Templates" → "Create New Template". Set up your template with these variables:
    • {{student_name}} — the student's name
    • {{topic}} — the essay topic
    • {{essay}} — the full essay text
    • {{word_count}} — the word count
    • {{submitted_at}} — submission date/time
    Note the Template ID (e.g. template_xyz789).
  4. Go to "Account" → "General" and copy your Public Key.
  5. Enter all three values in the form on the right and click "Save Settings".
Tip: The free plan allows 200 emails/month, which is enough for a typical class. Settings are saved in your browser so you only need to do this once.
